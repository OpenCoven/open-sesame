# Companion rail

The restart's window is a companion rail: a strip of icons that rests against a screen edge on the display the pointer is on, and opens into the panel beside it. It replaces the one ordinary window that the shortcut showed and hid. The work, its decisions and what a person still has to run are in [issue #161](https://github.com/OpenCoven/open-sesame/issues/161). The host side is [rail.rs](../src-tauri/src/rail.rs) with [panel.rs](../src-tauri/src/panel.rs) on macOS and [layer.rs](../src-tauri/src/layer.rs) on Wayland; the page side is [rail.js](../ui/rail.js), [attention.js](../ui/attention.js), [tokens.css](../ui/tokens.css) and [shell.css](../ui/shell.css). Read the [host instructions](../src-tauri/AGENTS.md) and the [shell contract](ui-shell.md) with it. Nothing here is shipped: the Swift host under `desktop/` remains the running application until the cutover in [epic #58](https://github.com/OpenCoven/open-sesame/issues/58).

## What a person sees

| State | Window | How it starts | How it ends |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Strip | 56 by 376, centred on the display's height, 8 from the edge, above other windows, on every workspace, never with the keyboard | Launch; closing the panel; the tray's Show or Hide when the rail was off the screen | The tray's Show or Hide takes it off the screen |
| Peek | 400 wide and the display's work area high, less 8 on each side, without the keyboard | The pointer rests on the strip for about a tenth of a second | The pointer leaves the panel for about a third of a second; a click inside turns it into a panel in use |
| Panel, open to stay | The same frame, with the keyboard | The shortcut, `open-sesame --toggle`, a second launch, the Dock icon, a click on a view or tool on the strip, the mark at the top of the strip | The same shortcut or command again, Escape, the control in the panel's bar, the mark; losing the keyboard when "Close the panel when it loses focus" is on |
| Wide panel | 860 wide | A view listed in `wideViews` (Shells) is shown; the control in the bar switches between wide and narrow | Another view is shown |

The strip's buttons do not move when the panel opens: the panel grows away from the edge, around the strip, and the strip stays centred on the display. Only a closed rail follows the pointer to another display, after the pointer has been there for about a tenth of a second; an open panel stays where it is. When the display the rail is on changes its scale and keeps its work area in pixels, the rail is placed again there within about two seconds (the displays are read again every 32 ticks), open or closed, wherever the pointer is. A scale change that also changes the work area, as a Windows taskbar that grows with the scale does, is not told apart from a new display: a closed strip is placed again once the pointer is on that display, and an open panel keeps its size until it closes. On macOS sizes are in points, so a scale change needs no new placement. (On Wayland the strip stays on its output; see [below](#where-it-is-a-rail).) A rail that was just closed under the pointer does not open again until the pointer has left it. A peek that was clicked closes when the window loses the keyboard, whatever the hide-on-blur setting says, unless something holds the window: a native dialog, a sign-in in the browser, a drag, or a pin. The default edge is the right one; Settings has the edge and whether a resting pointer opens the panel.

The strip is also where the companion says what needs a person. A view can carry one of two marks: something under way there (a running focus session) or something that needs the person (a finished focus session, a shell that ended while they were elsewhere). The mark on the top of the strip is the most urgent of them, and a status line for assistive technology names each view and why. A mark that needs the person is cleared when they open that view. Agents that need an answer are to use the same marks when [#79](https://github.com/OpenCoven/open-sesame/issues/79) lands; today the Agents view is a placeholder and sets none.

## Where it is a rail

| System | What runs | Go or no-go for the rail | Evidence so far |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| macOS | A borderless panel that does not activate the application, at the floating level, on every Space and over a full-screen application, with the popover material behind a see-through page | Go | Launched development build on a Mac with three displays, 18 November 2026: see [macOS](#macos) |
| Windows | A frameless window above the others, absent from the taskbar, that refuses the keyboard while it is the strip, with Mica on Windows 11 | Go for the strip, the panel and the keyboard on one display. Following to a second display has not been run, and a person's run is still owed | Launched development build on a hosted Windows Server 2025 virtual machine with one display, 18 November 2026: see [Windows](#windows). No person, and no real display or graphics card. The [owner's run](#the-owners-run-on-windows) lists what is left to look at, and the rail lines of the [smoke pass](desktop-smoke-pass.md) record it |
| Linux, X11 | The same window, see-through, with the page drawing the rounded corners; it refuses the keyboard while it is the strip | Go | Launched development build on a virtual X server with two monitors and a window manager, in a container, 18 November 2026: see [Linux, X11](#linux-x11). No real desktop, graphics card or person |
| Linux, Wayland with layer shell (KDE, Sway, Hyprland and other wlroots compositors) | The window as a layer surface docked against the edge, above other windows, through `libgtk-layer-shell`, which is opened if it is installed and never linked | Go for the docked strip and panel. No-go for following the pointer: the session picks the output and tells an application about the pointer only while it is over the application's own surface. The strip comes up on the output the session picks, and `open-sesame --toggle` brings the panel to the output in use | Launched development build under Sway 1.9 with two virtual outputs, in a container, 18 November 2026: see [Linux, Wayland](#linux-wayland). KDE and Hyprland have not been run |
| Linux, Wayland without layer shell (GNOME), or without the library | The ordinary window, opened by `open-sesame --toggle` bound in the desktop's keyboard settings | No-go for the rail: an application cannot place its window there at all. The ordinary window is the fallback | The same Sway session with `OPEN_SESAME_LAYER_SHELL=off`, which takes the same path as a session without the protocol. GNOME itself has not been run |

On Wayland the rail differs in three ways, all of them the session's rules. The strip does not follow the pointer from display to display: it stays on the output the session put it on, and a panel opened by a command (`open-sesame --toggle`, a second launch) leaves the screen for a moment and comes back on the output the session picks then, which under Sway is the one in use. A resting pointer still opens the panel and a pointer that leaves still closes it, because the session does say when the pointer is over the rail. And the keyboard is the session's to give: the strip never has it; a panel opened by resting the pointer gets it when a person clicks in it and loses it when they click elsewhere; a panel opened to stay takes the keyboard to itself, because a session gives a layer surface the keyboard in no other way without a click, and keeps it until the pointer has been over the panel and has left it, after which it is a click away like a peek's. A panel opened by a command and never visited by the pointer therefore keeps the keyboard from other windows until it is closed, with the same command or Escape. Global shortcuts are not available to an application on Wayland ([#108](https://github.com/OpenCoven/open-sesame/issues/108)), so the command is bound in the desktop's keyboard settings, as before. `OPEN_SESAME_LAYER_SHELL=off` gives the ordinary window in any build. Packaging ([#65](https://github.com/OpenCoven/open-sesame/issues/65)) decides whether `libgtk-layer-shell0` becomes a dependency of the package; without it the application starts and uses the ordinary window.

`GDK_BACKEND=x11` makes a Wayland session's window an X11 one through XWayland, and the application then takes the rail. That path is not checked and not recommended: XWayland tells an application where the pointer is only while it is over one of that application's windows, so the strip would not follow the pointer between displays.

## The page

The page is told how it is presented before any script runs: `window.__OPEN_SESAME_PRESENTATION__` is `rail` or `window`, and `window.__OPEN_SESAME_SURFACE__` is `material` (the system's material shows through; the page only tints it), `page` (the window is see-through with nothing behind it, and the page draws the rounded window) or `solid`. From then on it follows `rail.status` and the `rail.changed` host message, whose status is `{presentation, shown, expanded, held, wide, edge, hover}`, and asks for changes with `rail.set`, which takes any of `expanded`, `wide`, `edge` and `hover` and refuses anything else. The page never sizes or moves the window, and a refusal leaves it showing what the host still has.

The layout follows the window's own width and not a message, so the page is right in the same frame the host resizes the window in: at the strip's width only the strip is drawn. Views are drawn for one column of about 344 px and stay readable when the panel is wide or the window is an ordinary one, where the strip is a sidebar on the left and the panel fills the rest. Commands and Settings open as sheets over the view inside the panel; the view under them stays mounted. Escape closes a sheet first and then the panel; inside a terminal Escape belongs to the terminal.

Every colour, size, radius and duration is a token in [tokens.css](../ui/tokens.css). The theme is dark first, because the rail floats over other applications, with light values for a system set to light. Motion is decoration: the panel and the sheets fade in over 220 and 120 ms, nothing waits for an animation, and one rule turns all of it off under `prefers-reduced-motion`. The material is the system's where it offers one and the token colours elsewhere: the popover material on macOS, which follows the light or dark appearance, Mica on Windows 11, and a solid page on Linux. `OPEN_SESAME_MATERIAL=off` gives the solid page in any build. The material on macOS needs the web view to be see-through, which Tauri does through its `macos-private-api` feature; the application starts shells and so cannot be sandboxed for the Mac App Store in any case, and removing the feature and the `macOSPrivateApi` line in [tauri.conf.json](../src-tauri/tauri.conf.json) gives the solid page back.

## Checks and evidence

`cargo test -p open-sesame rail` covers the frame for each shape and edge and the pointer decisions (follow, peek, retreat, arming, an open panel staying put), the presentation for each session, the settings file and the `rail.set` request, all without a window. `node scripts/check-ui-rail.mjs` runs the page side against stand-ins and reads the facts both sides must agree on: the strip's size, the corner radius, the event and method names, colours and durations from tokens only, and an icon for every view. The [shell browser fixture](../scripts/fixtures/ui-shell-browser.mjs) has a rail step in Chromium and WebKit with a stand-in host: the strip alone at the closed size, a strip button the same distance from the screen edge and from the middle of the display before and after the panel opens, host-led open, wide, left edge and close, and an attention mark. None of these opens the application's window. A launched application is separate evidence, recorded per system below, and what a person's own pointer, keyboard and eyes find is separate again.

### macOS

A development build was launched on 18 November 2026 on a Mac with three displays (3840 by 1080 at scale 1 as the main display, 1728 by 1117 at scale 2 to its left, 1920 by 1200 at scale 1 above it), with its own bundle identifier and a disposable settings folder. Other people were using the Mac, so the pointer was not moved: at launch the rail went to the display the real pointer was on, and a stand-in pointer file (`OPEN_SESAME_DEV_POINTER`) then put it on each display in turn. The window list gave the strip at 56 by 376 against the right edge of each display, 8 in, at the floating level, and the points agreed with the frame the application had logged on the display at scale 2 as on the two at scale 1. A stand-in rest on the strip opened the panel at 400 by 1033 and a stand-in move away closed it. `open-sesame --toggle` opened the panel to stay and closed it. Each view was opened by an accessibility press addressed to the application's process: Today, Projects, Focus and Agents at 400 wide and Shells at 860, back to 400 for the next view; Settings and Commands opened as sheets; the bar's control closed the panel. The window alone was captured at each step. Not looked at: a person's own pointer resting on the strip, the shortcut pressed on a keyboard, a full-screen application behind the panel, the tray menu, and the light appearance.

### Linux, X11

A development build was launched on 18 November 2026 in an Ubuntu 24.04 container (arm64, in a virtual machine on the Mac; WebKitGTK 2.52.6, GTK 3.24.41) on an X server with the dummy video driver and two 1920 by 1080 monitors side by side, with Openbox as the window manager, a terminal as the other application and a stand-in for a desktop's tray. The pointer was the X server's own, moved with `xdotool`. This is a virtual display: no real desktop environment, graphics card, compositor or person, and software rendering.

What the window list, the keyboard focus and the application's log gave, in order: the strip at 56 by 376 against the right edge of the monitor the pointer was on (x 1856 on the left one, 3776 on the right one, y 352) with the keyboard still in the terminal; the strip on the other monitor after the pointer moved there; the panel at 400 by 1064 after the pointer rested on the strip, keyboard still in the terminal, and the strip again after it left; `open-sesame --toggle` opening the panel with the keyboard in it, the open panel staying on its monitor while the pointer went to the other, and the same command closing it with the keyboard back in the terminal and the strip following the pointer; a click on a view on the strip opening the panel to stay with the keyboard, and Escape closing it and giving the keyboard back; the tray's Show or Hide taking the window off the screen and putting the strip back; a panel open to stay remaining open after a click in another window; a peek that was clicked taking the keyboard and closing after a click in another window; one page load throughout; and no process left after the tray's Quit. Not looked at: a compositor (the see-through corners were drawn on black), a desktop's own panel reserving space, mixed scale factors, and a real tray.

### Linux, Wayland

The same build was launched in the same container under Sway 1.9 with two headless 1920 by 1080 outputs and software rendering, with gtk-layer-shell 0.8.2, a terminal as the other application, a virtual pointer and a virtual keyboard. No real desktop, graphics card, KDE, Hyprland, GNOME or person.

Before the product code was written, a probe in a scratch copy answered the question this work started with: the GTK window Tauri makes can be turned into a layer surface as long as that happens before it is first shown. Docked that way it was a 56 by 376 strip against the right edge with see-through corners, grew to 400 by 1064 when tied to the top and bottom as well, moved to a named output, and came back on the output in use when hidden and shown with no output named. Keyboard modes under Sway: with none, a click reached the page and typing stayed in the terminal; with on-demand, typing reached the page after a click in it and the terminal after a click there; with exclusive, typing reached the page at once and kept reaching it after a click in the terminal; going from exclusive to on-demand gave the keyboard back to the terminal.

The product build then gave, in order: no ordinary window, and the strip as a layer surface with typing still reaching the terminal; the panel after the pointer rested on the strip, typing still in the terminal, and the strip after it left; a click on Projects on the strip opening the panel to stay, and text typed after a click in its field arriving there; typing reaching the terminal again after the pointer left the panel and clicked there, and the panel's field again after a click in it; `open-sesame --toggle` closing the panel; a peek that was clicked keeping the panel open with the keyboard and closing after a click in the terminal; with the second output in use, `open-sesame --toggle` opening the panel on the second output with typed text arriving at once, and closing it; the tray's Show or Hide twice; one page load throughout; and no process left after the tray's Quit. Started with `OPEN_SESAME_LAYER_SHELL=off` in the same session, the application made the ordinary window, started no pointer thread, and `open-sesame --toggle` hid and showed it.

Two things seen there belong to the check and to Sway and not to a person's desktop, and are written down so that nobody mistakes them: each typed string came from a virtual keyboard that was added and removed, and the window was told it lost and regained the keyboard each time, which would close a panel set to close when it loses focus; and on an output with no other window Sway had nothing to give the keyboard to, so it told the window only later that the strip had given the keyboard up, and a peek that was clicked there stayed open after a click on the empty desktop. Not looked at: KDE, Hyprland, a real pointer and keyboard, a panel or bar that reserves space, scale factors other than 1, the left edge, and the wide panel.

### Windows

The rail has been started on Windows only on a hosted virtual machine. No person has used it there.

Launched: the Rust job of the hosted workflow starts the development binary it has just built on the Windows runner's desktop ([rail-windows.ps1](../scripts/fixtures/rail-windows.ps1)), beside a window of its own that has the keyboard, and writes down the window's place and which window is in front. Resting on the strip is a stand-in pointer file; the click is a real one. The runner is Windows Server 2025 (build 26100) with one 1024 by 768 display at 100%, WebView2 and no graphics card.

The first run, [44318847607](https://github.com/OpenCoven/open-sesame/actions/runs/44318847607) at `e2b4c4e`, found two faults. The window was 8 pixels too far right, its edge off the screen: a window with a shadow and no frame keeps the unseen border a frame has for resizing, and a position is that border's corner. And a panel opened by resting on the strip never closed: on Windows the window's focus events follow the web view inside it, which said it had the keyboard as soon as it existed while another window was in front, so the rail took the panel for one a person was typing in. Both are fixed in `rail.rs`: the border is taken off the position, and the rail asks Windows which window is in front.

The second run, [44320756366](https://github.com/OpenCoven/open-sesame/actions/runs/44320756366) at `d5728b4`, gave, in order: the strip at 56 by 376, 8 pixels in from the right edge and centred in the work area, above other windows, refusing the keyboard, with the other window still in front; the panel at 400 by 704 after the stand-in pointer rested on the strip, the other window still in front, and the strip again after it left; a real click on the first view on the strip opening the panel to stay with the rail's window in front, and staying open when the pointer left; `open-sesame --toggle` closing it with the other window in front again; the same command opening the panel with the keyboard and closing it with the keyboard given back; one page load and one process throughout; and no process left when the check stopped it. The screen images kept with the run show the strip and the panel with rounded corners and a shadow, and no taskbar button for the rail.

Not looked at: a second display, so the strip following the pointer on Windows; scale factors other than 100%; the shortcut pressed as a key; the tray icon; Mica (the images show a plain light surface, and nothing says whether that is Mica on this server edition); a full-screen application; the left edge; the wide panel; and anything a person would notice that a window's place and the front window do not say.

Compilation and tests: in the same job, `cargo fmt --all --check`, `cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets --locked -- -D warnings`, `cargo test --workspace --locked` and `cargo build --package open-sesame --locked` ended with success on the MSVC toolchain (Rust 1.95.0). Locally, `cargo check` and `cargo clippy -- -D warnings` for the target `x86_64-pc-windows-gnu` ended with status 0 from the Linux container, before and after the two fixes; nothing was linked or run there.

### The owner's run on Windows

No person has run the rail on Windows. Build the development binary and start it with a disposable settings folder, as the [smoke pass](desktop-smoke-pass.md#owner-instructions) describes, and write down what happens at each step; steps 1 to 8 are that checklist's C1, C2, C7 and R1 to R6. The hosted run above already showed steps 1, 3 and 5 on one display with a stand-in pointer; steps 2, 4 and 6 to 10 have not been run in any form.

1. Start the application with the pointer on one display. The strip appears against the right edge of that display, centred on its height, with rounded corners and no taskbar button. The window you were typing in still has the keyboard.
2. Move the pointer to a second display and wait a moment. The strip moves to that display's right edge. Do it with displays at different scale settings if you have them, and say whether the strip is the same size on each. Then change the scale of the display the strip is on in the system's display settings: within a moment the strip is the same size to the eye again, against the same edge.
3. Rest the pointer on the strip. The panel opens beside it without taking the keyboard, and the icons under the pointer do not move. Move the pointer away. The panel closes.
4. Press Control+Alt+Shift+Space. The panel opens and typing goes into it. Press it again. The panel closes and typing goes into the window you were in before; say so if it does not: the hosted run showed this for `open-sesame --toggle`, not for a key.
5. Click a view on the strip without waiting for the panel. The panel opens on that view, to stay.
6. Left click the tray icon. The panel opens; click it again and it closes. Right click it and choose Show or Hide. The strip leaves the screen; choose it again and it comes back.
7. Open Shells. The panel widens. Press the control with the two arrows in the bar. It narrows, and the shell still fits its column.
8. In Settings, change the edge to Left. The strip and the panel move to the left edge and mirror.
9. Look at the panel's background on Windows 11. It should be Mica: the desktop's colours tinted through it while the panel has the keyboard. If it is black, see-through or flickers, start the application with `OPEN_SESAME_MATERIAL=off` and say what you saw.
10. Put another application in full screen and repeat steps 3 and 4.
